ℹ️ What this app does

This tool lets you simulate how a hypothetical investment in a stock market index would have performed if you had started at any month in its history: from the S&P 500 (back to 1927) to the Nikkei 225, European and emerging-market indices. You can model a single lump sum, regular contributions, or a mix of both, and compare up to three scenarios side by side. The data is refreshed automatically every day.

How to use it

  1. In Settings (☰ on a phone), choose an index and whether you want results in Nominal or Inflation-adjusted terms.
  2. Set up one or more scenarios: Lump sum (invest once at the start), DCA (Dollar-Cost Averaging: the same amount on a regular schedule), Escalated DCA (the amount rises each year by your escalation percentage) or Custom.
  3. Pick the time window on the historical chart: choose a length (5–50 years, full history or custom). The chart shows only that window; tap or drag on the full-history strip below it to move the window, drag across the main chart to zoom in, or use the slider and ◀ ▶ buttons. Untick Use the time window from the chart in a scenario to type its own start date and duration.
  4. Choose the contribution frequency: yearly, quarterly or monthly.
  5. The app shows the final value, the total invested, the growth, the annualized return (CAGR or IRR) and the maximum drawdown, plus charts and a results table.
  6. Download any scenario's monthly data as a CSV.

Important: all results are price return only and exclude dividends. Returns are in each index's own currency; inflation adjustment uses the inflation of that currency area.

Return

Yearly returns

Each bar shows the portfolio's change over one calendar year. Green = a positive year, red = a negative year. Contributions made during the year are included in the portfolio value, so a green year does not necessarily mean the market went up: it means your portfolio ended the year higher than it started.

Results table

What the columns mean
  • Invested: total money you put in (initial + all contributions).
  • Final value: what your portfolio is worth at the end.
  • Net gain: final value minus what you invested.
  • Total growth: the same profit, as a percentage of what you invested.
  • CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate): the average yearly growth of a single amount invested once at the start: (final ÷ invested)^(1 ÷ years) − 1. Shown only for a pure lump sum.
  • IRR (Internal Rate of Return): the average yearly return when you deposit money at different moments: the one fixed yearly rate at which all deposits, each growing from its own date, add up to the final value. For a lump sum it equals the CAGR.
  • ↳ rows: the comparisons (Compare with): the same deposits growing at a fixed yearly rate, or kept as cash with 0% interest.
  • Max DD (Maximum Drawdown): the biggest drop from a peak to a later low.
